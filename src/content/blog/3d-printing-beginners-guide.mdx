3D printing is no longer exclusive to labs and large industries. Today, anyone can access this technology and create physical objects from a digital design. In this guide, we explain everything you need to know to get started.

## What is 3D printing?

3D printing (also called additive manufacturing) is a process that builds three-dimensional objects layer by layer from a digital model. Unlike traditional machining that "subtracts" material, 3D printing "adds" material exactly where needed.

## Main technologies

### FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling)

The most popular for home use and prototyping. Works by melting a plastic filament and depositing it in layers. This is the technology we primarily use at Vectra.

**Advantages:**
- Accessible cost
- Wide variety of materials
- Easy to use and maintain

**Disadvantages:**
- Visible layer lines
- Support needed for overhangs

### SLA (Stereolithography)

Uses photosensitive liquid resin cured by UV light. Produces parts with very smooth surfaces and high detail resolution.

**Ideal for:** jewelry, high-detail figures, dental parts.

## Most common materials (FDM)

### PLA (Polylactic Acid)
The star material for beginners. Easy to print, biodegradable, and available in hundreds of colors. Ideal for decorative objects, figures, and prototypes.

### PETG
Combines the ease of PLA with greater mechanical resistance and temperature tolerance. Perfect for functional parts that don't require technical materials.

### TPU / TPE
Flexible rubber-like materials. Excellent for phone cases, soles, gaskets, and any application requiring flexibility.

## The printing process

1. **CAD Design**: Create or download the 3D model (STL, OBJ, STEP)
2. **Slicing**: Convert the 3D model to printer instructions
3. **Preparation**: Level the bed, load filament, verify parameters
4. **Printing**: The process can last from minutes to days
5. **Post-processing**: Remove supports, sand, paint, join pieces
